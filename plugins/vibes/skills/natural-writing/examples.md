# Natural writing examples

These pairs show reusable editing decisions. The names, amount, and project details are fictional. Copy the judgment, not the wording.

## 1. Turn stacked facts into a developing thought

### Before

```text
Sam was originally brought in to lead that side of the project, but his work didn't meet the standard we expected. When you offered to take it over, we appreciated that and agreed to move forward with you leading it. You've put a lot of work into it, but we still don't have the design leadership and direction we expected from the beginning. Chris has still had to carry much of the planning, concepts, and visual direction himself, and we're now further into the project without a clear design foundation in place.
```

### After

```text
As you know, Sam was originally brought in to lead that side of the project, and when that didn't work out, you stepped in and offered to take it over. We really appreciated you taking responsibility, and we wanted to give that approach a fair chance. Unfortunately, even with all the time and effort you've put into it, we still haven't gotten the level of design leadership and direction we expected. Chris has continued to carry much of the planning, concepts, and visual direction himself, and that's left us further into the project without the foundation we hoped to have by now.
```

### Why it works

The revision treats the history as shared context instead of presenting evidence against the recipient. `Unfortunately` connects sincere appreciation to an outcome neither side wanted. The criticism remains explicit, and the final clause explains the consequence.

## 2. Give a hard constraint a natural entry

### Before

```text
We do need to figure out how that fits into the budget we've already committed. As we've talked about before, $30K is the full budget for the project and a hard limit for us. A big part of why we made that commitment was to have the design and creative direction handled without Chris needing to be so involved.
```

### After

```text
Before we go there, though, we need to come back to the budget conversation we've already had. As we've talked about before, $30K is a hard limit for us, and it was a commitment we made largely so Chris wouldn't have to carry the design and creative direction himself.
```

### Why it works

The first sentence connects the positive idea under discussion to the constraint that must be resolved before it can happen. The second joins the amount and the reason for it, so the budget does not arrive as an isolated ultimatum.

## 3. Remove proof that adds tension but no information

### Before

```text
It also lines up with what you said recently about the design needing a stronger hand and your own strengths leaning more toward engineering.
```

### After

Delete the sentence.

### Why it works

The recipient already knows what he said, and the surrounding message already makes the role change clear. Repeating the statement would add tension without changing what the reader understands or needs to do.

## 4. Connect ordinary facts without adding warmth

### Before

```text
The migration completed at 2:10 AM. Error rates increased at 2:18 AM. We paused the rollout at 2:24 AM. No customer data was lost. Twelve customers saw failed requests.
```

### After

```text
The migration completed at 2:10 AM, but error rates increased at 2:18 AM, so we paused the rollout at 2:24 AM. No customer data was lost, although twelve customers saw failed requests.
```

### Why it works

Time, contrast, cause, and qualification provide all the connection this update needs. Adding relational warmth would make a factual status note sound artificial.

## 5. Attach concrete conditions directly to their outcome

### Before

```text
We reviewed the missed records, and the addresses were generally valid. Many appear to be recently created properties where our property and ownership records have not yet been fully populated. In other cases, we did not have a complete owner mailing address or a reliable person associated with that address. Because we only return results when we can confidently connect the correct person to the property, those records were marked as missed hits rather than returning uncertain information.
```

### After

```text
We reviewed the missed records, and while the addresses were generally valid, many appear to be in newer developments where the property and ownership information isn't fully available yet. In other cases, a complete owner mailing address wasn't available, or our system couldn't reliably connect a person to the address, so the record resulted in a missed hit rather than potentially returning the wrong contact.
```

### Why it works

The first sentence keeps the qualification with the finding. In the second, the incomplete information flows directly into the missed-hit outcome. The standing confidence policy is not lost; it is expressed through the concrete alternative avoided: returning the wrong contact. Keeping it as a separate `Because we only return...` sentence would recreate the detached cause-policy-outcome chain.

## 6. Preserve mixed tense while connecting facts

### Before

```text
We checked the invoices. The totals were accurate. Several invoices used new tax categories. Our accounting system does not support those categories yet. Those invoices require manual review. We expect to finish tomorrow.
```

### After

```text
We checked the invoices, and while the totals were accurate, several used new tax categories that our accounting system doesn't support yet. Those invoices require manual review, which we expect to finish tomorrow.
```

### Why it works

The completed check and invoice contents remain in the past, while the system limitation, review requirement, and expected completion remain current. Connecting the ideas does not authorize changing when they happened.

## 7. Remove mannered prose without banning metaphor

### Before

```text
The revised handoff gives the launch plan a firmer spine and creates a cleaner runway for support, which should keep small questions from becoming speed bumps once customers start coming through the door.
```

### After

```text
The revised handoff gives the launch plan a stronger structure and makes it easier for support to prepare, which should keep small questions from causing delays once customers start using the product.
```

### Why it works

The original makes the reader translate several decorative images back into ordinary meaning, and it imports a storefront image that does not fit a software launch. The revision states the meaning directly. That does not make every metaphor a problem: a comparison such as `The setup should feel like a guided tour, not a scavenger hunt` earns its place because it makes the desired experience easier to understand.

## What not to learn from these examples

- Do not add `as you know` unless the history is genuinely shared.
- Do not place `unfortunately` before every criticism or add warmth to a factual update.
- Do not force every paragraph into two long compound sentences.
- Do not delete a proposal, request, commitment, or boundary merely to make a message feel more open-ended.
- Do not dissolve a policy sentence when announcing, defending, or changing the policy is the point.
- Do not remove a metaphor merely because it is figurative; remove it when a literal phrase conveys the point more clearly.
- Do not preserve awkward syntax or every rationale clause when the user asked for a cleanup; preserve material meaning and voice.
- Do not add a consequence unless the source supports it.
- Do not reuse the example's names, roles, budget, or subject matter unless they fit the actual message.
